# DEEC evidence and validation plan

**Status:** Proposed. This document extends the [thermodynamic simulation plan](thermodynamic-simulation-plan.md). It records evidence from the four additional documents and defines the corresponding checks for the C++ model.

### PW-100 comparisons from TM 84903

Start with a dry intermediate-power case from the publication, once enough of its boundary and actuator data can be recovered. Fig. 6 is a candidate source for the inlet recovery curve used by this deck. Record it as an airframe/inlet assumption for that comparison and confirm the plotted correction convention. Extract the comparison curves by figure, power regime, altitude, test article and series. Fig. 14 only covers a specified maximum-power comparison at 40,000 ft. The paper describes slow maneuvers as effectively steady, so the curves are suitable for quasi-steady checks.

### Partial calibration and station data in CP 2298

- **CIVV schedule:** Paper 4 Fig. 10, p. 68 plots vane angle against corrected fan speed and distinguishes the F100(3) and F100 EMD schedules. Digitize the matching curve with its original units and variant label. Figs. 11–12 show operating slices from XD11-10 vane tests. These local slices cannot determine fan pressure ratio, flow and efficiency over the map.
- **Inlet pressure estimation:** Paper 5 p. 79 plots a low-distortion `PT2/PS2` correlation versus corrected airflow as a percentage. The flight and facility series were obtained using different engines. This is a check or partial fit for one lookup. The two-dimensional corrected-airflow pumping map required by p. 78 is still missing.
- **Augmentor fuel metering:** Paper 11 pp. 172–176 separates core and fan-duct spray rings. The p. 174 diagram derives duct airflow from total minus core airflow and meters each fuel stream using its channel-specific fuel-air target and air mass flow. Treat this as a basis for stream-resolved metering after the lumped thermodynamic model. The published logic describes separate targets but does not tabulate their surfaces.
- **Station numbering:** Paper 13 p. 218 provides the PW-100 crosswalk. Station 2 is engine inlet, 2.5 is rear compressor inlet, 3 is rear compressor exit, 4 is combustor exit, 4.5 is fan-turbine inlet, 5 is fan-turbine exit, 6 is augmentor inlet, 7 is nozzle throat and 8 is nozzle exit. Preserve the distinction between geometric locations and modeled planes. Use pp. 219–222 to compare measurement locations and the empirical thrust model before substituting an internal result for a measured channel.

## DEEC in the C++ design

1. **Gas path.** Run deterministic component evaluations within the algebraic matching solve. The normal inputs include ambient/inlet state and actual fuel flows, nozzle area, vane positions, and bleed position. A dry run supplies zero augmentor fuel. A prescribed-input replay measures gas-path response independently of DEEC schedule uncertainty.
2. **Sensors and state.** Convert solved station states and rotor speeds into a signal snapshot. The first DEEC prototype can consume `Pt2` directly. CP 2298 Paper 5, p. 78 documents an iterative estimator. Given `N1c` and a trial EPR, the engine pumping curve returns corrected airflow `Wac2`. A second lookup `R(Wac2)` returns `Pt2/Ps2`. An isolated scalar solve can close `Pt2 - Ps2 * R(Wac2(N1c, Pt6M/Pt2)) = 0` once both correlations are available. Keep static `PS2`, mixed total `PT6M`, and temperature station FTIT separate.
3. **Controller.** Consume the sensor snapshot and throttle command. Evaluate a versioned set of mode-dependent schedules and limit tables. Track fuel command, vane and area commands, along with mode, active limiter, and schedule validity. Start with a synthetic PI and a deterministic test fixture with known settings. Transfer to an identified historical profile only when the schedules, limits, units, gain conventions and version are known.
4. **Actuators and orchestrator.** Keep requested commands distinct from achieved fuel flows and geometry. In a dynamic run, advance controller state at its declared sample instants, hold commands between instants, and integrate shafts and modeled actuators. Re-solve the algebraic matches at each integration evaluation. A failed gas-path match invalidates the result for that evaluation. It cannot be hidden by advancing controller state or by using a one-step lag in the flow balances.
5. **Calibration package.** Record each schedule's axes, units, validity, transition logic and source. Track firm data separately from bounded fits and synthetic fixtures in the output metadata. Missing schedules in a historical-profile run should produce a diagnostic. A prescribed-input run remains available without controller tables.

CP 2298 Paper 12, p. 211 describes a **0.02 s DEEC minor computation cycle** and a **0.005 s integration interval** in its study model. Use those values as metadata in a reproduction of that specific experiment. Plant integration, controller sampling and reported recording rates are distinct. TM 84903 p. 4 describes different recording rates for DEEC data, most other channels, and selected fast channels. Neither source establishes a default time step for the new C++ implementation.

### Order of validation

- **Plant only:** Verify station balances, map behavior and convergence with analytic and synthetic dry fixtures. Add imposed wet fuel and geometry after the dry cycle closes.
- **Replay:** Digitize a trace with identifiable boundaries and measured actuator histories. Begin with a dry PW-100 operating case from TM 84903. Record unavailable replay inputs and restrict this comparison to outputs the evidence supports.
- **Controller only:** Exercise steady-state targets, lower and upper limits, mode changes, sensor timing, and actuator saturation against a deterministic synthetic plant. Each historical schedule remains a separately versioned input.
- **Integrated dynamics:** Replay selected Paper 11 and 12 cases after the required calibration exists. Compare trends and timings with the corresponding phase, separating plant solve error from integration, actuator, and control timing effects. Refine integration steps and measure convergence rate, failure cases, and execution latency.
